## Purpose

This directory preserves the trading evidence associated with the Genesis DELL Market Lab.

The purpose is to document what was actually traded, when it was traded, what risk framework was used, what information was available at the time, and what outcome occurred.

Trade evidence must remain separate from later interpretation.

## Core Rule

Do not reconstruct a trade from memory when a receipt exists.

Do not replace the original trade plan with the eventual outcome.

Do not silently correct an entry, exit, stop, or timestamp.

If new evidence changes a previously recorded value, preserve both records and document the correction.

## Classification

REAL TRADE

A position executed with actual capital.

PAPER TRADE

A simulated position with no real capital at risk.

OBSERVATIONAL SETUP

A setup documented without an executed position.

PROPOSED TRADE

A trade plan documented before execution.

## Research Principle

The quality of a trade is not determined only by whether it made money.

A profitable trade can have poor process.

A losing trade can have sound process.

The Market Lab evaluates both process and outcome.

## Relationship to $DLAB

DELL trading activity must be analyzed independently from $DLAB activity.

The existence of a profitable DELL trade does not establish that $DLAB will appreciate.

The existence of a losing DELL trade does not establish that $DLAB will decline.

The two markets may later be compared, but their evidence records remain separate.

## Benchmark

For the DELL Market Lab, the primary market benchmark is the reference DELL position.

When $DLAB becomes active, additional comparisons may include:

Holding DELL.

Trading DELL.

Holding $DLAB.

Converting $DLAB back into DELL.

DELL-equivalent performance.

USD-equivalent performance.

## Provenance Doctrine

The trade receipt is evidence.

The livestream is evidence.

The timestamp is evidence.

The later explanation is interpretation.

Preserve them separately.
